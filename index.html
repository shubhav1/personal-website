Projects

flash-attention-2 FlashAttention-2, written in Triton from scratch

Custom Triton kernels for FlashAttention-2 forward and backward passes, wrapped as a PyTorch autograd function and autotuned per sequence length, head dim, and causality, then verified for correctness against a naive scaled dot-product attention implementation written from scratch. Wired it into a transformer's attention layer and built out a full benchmarking suite on an A100 — CUDA memory snapshots tracking peak usage across context lengths, and NSight Systems traces to see which kernels actually dominate a training step.

  • Triton
  • memory profiling
  • NSight profiling
my-gpt A decoder-only transformer, rebuilt one modern improvement at a time

Started as a from-scratch GPT following Karpathy's Zero to Hero series, then turned into a testbed for modern transformer changes — BPE tokenization, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE — each isolated in its own controlled ablation study on Tiny Shakespeare. Diagnosed a stubborn overfitting problem by working out the model's tokens-per-parameter ratio against Chinchilla scaling laws, and traced an RMSNorm slowdown that contradicted the literature back to a missing torch.compile call, which turned into its own follow-up investigation.

  • PyTorch
  • 8 ablation studies
  • best val bpb 2.12
mlp-from-scratch A word-level language model, built entirely in NumPy

Inspired by Karpathy's makemore: a hand-rolled embedding table, linear layers, BatchNorm, and softmax cross-entropy, with every forward and backward pass derived and implemented from scratch — no autograd. Trained and compared four architectures on Tiny Shakespeare, methodically debugging tanh saturation, BatchNorm EMA drift, and a stop-token bug by tracing distributions and running stats layer-by-layer rather than guessing at fixes.

  • NumPy
  • from scratch
  • test loss 6.28

About Me

I'm Shubha, a student at USC majoring in Artificial Intelligence and Business with a minor in Mathematics. My whole life, I've been attracted to really hard problems, whether it was coding USACO algorithms back in middle school, sewing my dream top with limited fabric and even fewer sewing skills, or consulting for the biggest problems at the biggest companies at McKinsey.

Naturally, this led me to machine learning. I spend most of my time learning about, coding, and implementing machine learning solutions, from studying the original transformer paper to building my own models. I'm working towards a future where I can meaningfully contribute to the field of ML research and development.

If I'm not learning or coding, you'll probably find me exploring new parts of LA with my friends, thrift-flipping a new outfit, or playing with my oil pastels.
